# User Interviews — Playbook + Log

You've never done user research interviews before. This file teaches you the basics and tracks the work.

---

## The 30-second philosophy

You're not asking people what they want — you're asking them what they did, what hurt, and what they tried. People are terrible at predicting their own behavior, great at remembering specific past experiences.

**Bad question:** "Would you use an app like crystal?"
**Good question:** "Tell me about the last time you tried to process a difficult emotion."

## How many you need

**5.** That's it. Jakob Nielsen's research shows 5 interviews surfaces ~85% of usability issues. For exploratory research, 5 is enough to see patterns. You're not running a clinical trial — you're calibrating intuition.

After 5, if everyone is saying the same thing, you have signal. If everyone is saying different things, you need to tighten your recruitment criteria (you're talking to too varied a group).

## Recruiting

You don't have a budget. Use these channels in order:

1. **Your network.** Post on LinkedIn / Instagram: "I'm doing 5 user research interviews for a wellness product I'm building. 30 min via Zoom. DM me if you're interested." Friends will refer friends. This gets you 2–3.
2. **Reddit.** r/journaling, r/Mindfulness, r/getmotivated. Mods often allow research recruitment if you ask first and offer to share findings.
3. **Discord servers.** Mental wellness, productivity, self-improvement servers. Same rules — ask the mods first.
4. **Twitter/X.** Same pitch as LinkedIn. Lower yield but you may hit your target audience faster.

**Filter for the right people.** You don't need just anyone. You need people who have *tried to journal or reflect* in the past 6 months — successfully or not. The "not" people are actually your most valuable interviewees. They'll tell you what's broken.

**Don't pay** unless you have to. Genuine interest is a better filter than money. If you must compensate, $20 Amazon gift cards are standard.

## The session structure

30 minutes. Zoom recording on (with permission). Otter.ai or Whisper for transcription afterward.

**Opening (3 min)**
- Thank them. Tell them what you're working on at a high level (one sentence).
- Say: "There are no right or wrong answers. I want your real experience, not what you think I want to hear."
- Ask permission to record. Tell them you won't share their name publicly.

**Past behavior (15 min) — the core**
- Tell me about the last time you tried to reflect on a hard emotion. Walk me through it.
- What did you do? How did it feel?
- What apps or tools have you tried? Walk me through one of them.
- Why did you stop using it?
- When in your day, if ever, do you have time and headspace to journal?

**Current state (7 min)**
- What's your current way of processing difficult feelings? (Could be nothing — that's data.)
- What works about it? What doesn't?
- If a friend asked you for advice on emotional self-reflection, what would you say?

**Probing the future (5 min) — careful here**
- Don't ask "would you use crystal?" That gets you polite lies.
- Ask: "If you had a tool that did X (the one specific feature you want to test), what would have to be true for you to actually open it daily?"

**Close**
- Anything else you want to share?
- Can I follow up if I have one or two questions?

## What NOT to do

- **Don't pitch your product.** You're a researcher, not a salesperson. They should leave knowing almost nothing about crystal's actual features.
- **Don't ask "would you" or "do you think you would".** These predict zero behavior.
- **Don't lead.** "Don't you think reflection is important?" → throw out.
- **Don't fill silence.** When they pause, wait. Most of the gold comes after the awkward 4-second pause when they keep going on their own.
- **Don't talk more than 20% of the time.** If you're talking more, you're interviewing yourself.

## Synthesizing 5 interviews

After all 5 are done, in one sitting:

1. **Read all 5 transcripts.** Highlight quotes that surprised you or that you heard from multiple people.
2. **Cluster the highlights.** Group similar ones. Common clusters: "what makes me start", "what makes me stop", "what would bring me back", "what I'm secretly worried about".
3. **Name each cluster.** One short phrase per cluster.
4. **Find the contradictions.** Where did people disagree? Those are the interesting decision points.
5. **Write up 3 things:**
   - The biggest pattern you saw (1 paragraph)
   - The most surprising thing someone said (1 quote + context)
   - One specific change to crystal you'd make based on what you heard (1 paragraph)

That writeup IS the portfolio case study. Aim for 600–800 words. Show the work, not just the conclusion.

## Resources (read 30 min each, that's enough)

- **The Mom Test** by Rob Fitzpatrick — short book, the gold standard on customer interviews. If you read one thing, read this. 150 pages.
- **"What is User Research?"** — Nielsen Norman Group has a free 20-minute primer.
- **Teresa Torres's "Continuous Discovery Habits"** — frameworks for ongoing research, but the first 50 pages are enough for now.
